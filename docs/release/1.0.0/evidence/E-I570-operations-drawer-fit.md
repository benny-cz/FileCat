# I570: Operations drawer fits the visible work

2026-10-30. Preliminary source-level UX correction at `e1eeada5`. The owner supplied a screenshot of one completed delete with a large empty lower half of the open Operations drawer. It is an observation of the current UI, not a final installed-candidate acceptance.

The drawer had a fixed 300-DIP default height. Its job list and selected report share a row, so a single short result stretched the list into unused space. The correction sizes an uncustomized drawer from the visible history rows and selected detail/issue content, bounded by the available workspace. More rows or detail increase it; recovery content keeps its existing usable minimum. The resize handle still overrides automatic sizing and saves the user's chosen height. A previously saved nondefault height remains a manual preference.

Focused `OperationsDrawerLayoutTests` passed **14/14**, including the new one-completed-job, six-job growth, manual override and saved-height checks. Existing small-window, interrupted-recovery Stop visibility, keyboard and pointer resize, and workspace-restore cases passed in the same run. The one-job regression requires 140–230 DIP, six rows at least 60 DIP taller, and at least 90 DIP of remaining workspace. The test does not substitute for a visual check of the exact installed Windows 1.0.0 candidate.

**Stopping rule:** On the exact installed Windows 1.0.0 candidate, complete one short job and open Operations: no large blank lower region, its report actions visible, and the main workspace usable. Add several jobs and a selected issue or recovery item: the drawer grows within the workspace bound and its scrollable controls remain reachable. Manually resize it, close and reopen the drawer, and verify the chosen height persists. Reconcile producer and four platform CI jobs and any new skips at the latest product source. Public preview.3 predates this change; stable 1.0.0 remains NO-GO until the separate candidate and signing gates pass.
